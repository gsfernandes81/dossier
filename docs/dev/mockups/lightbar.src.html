A Lighter Bar

The bar as it ships is ANSI 8 with bright-white text — a mid grey. Two lighter pairs, both with black text, drawn in Termux's own default colours rather than yours: black background, and the classic xterm palette Termux ships with.

The three bars

As it ships

bg ANSI 8 · fg ANSI 15

A mid grey. The hints are dim white on it, which is the combination you flagged: two greys close together.

Light

bg ANSI 7 · fg ANSI 0

Near-white, black text. The dim hints become grey on light instead of grey on grey, which is a real contrast rather than a near-miss — and the armed warning can use a dark red that a light background actually supports.

Brightest

bg ANSI 15 · fg ANSI 0

Pure white against a pure black screen — the highest contrast available, and the same pair the selected row already uses. Which is the objection: the bar would look exactly like a selection.

In place

Whole screens, so the bar is judged against the list above it rather than on its own.

Two things this mockup is guessing at. The palette above is Termux's built-in default — the classic xterm scheme — from memory, not from your device. If you have ever set colors.properties, or if the built-in default has moved, the greys here are wrong.

And dim is `SGR 2`, which not every terminal honours. If Termux ignores it, every "dim" element in this app — the locators under each document, the hints, the placeholder — is rendering at full brightness and has been all along, which would change what a light bar needs to do. Both are one command to settle:

# is dim actually dimmer?
printf '\e[2mdim\e[0m  normal  \e[1mbold\e[0m\n'
# and the three bar candidates, side by side
printf '\e[48;5;8m\e[38;5;15m  ANSI 8 on 15  \e[0m\n'
printf '\e[48;5;7m\e[38;5;0m  ANSI 7 on 0   \e[0m\n'
printf '\e[48;5;15m\e[38;5;0m  ANSI 15 on 0  \e[0m\n'

What I would take

ANSI 7 with black text, if either. It gets the contrast you are after without taking the one pair the selection already owns — and a status line that looks like a selected row is a worse problem than a status line that is slightly too quiet.

The cost is that it inverts the polarity risk rather than removing it. ANSI 0 was unusable on your theme because it matched your background; ANSI 7 would be unusable on a light theme for the same reason. Neither is wrong — it just means whichever we pick, the band is tuned to one polarity and merely survives the other.
