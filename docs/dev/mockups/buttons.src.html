Four vocabularies

Same tiling, same field, same list above. Only what is written inside the three cells changes — and, with it, what the hint line has left to teach.

K Keys

as it ships now · hints carry ⏎ and ^q

The button shows the key a keyboard would press. It teaches the binding every time you look at it, which is worth something on a device you drive both ways.

It also shows ^x and ^t to a thumb that has no control key — two of the three buttons are labelled in a language the toucher cannot speak.

G Glyph + word

keys move to the hint line

A touch-shaped mark instead of a key: › for the record, ! for expiry — the same mark the expired rows already carry — and ⌕ for searching inside scans.

Reads as an app rather than as a cheat sheet. But it spends a column on decoration in a cell that has a word in it already, and it puts a third glyph in the font's way.

W Words recommended

nothing but the label

The cell is thirteen columns and the longest word is six. A centred word with room around it is the most legible thing this bar can hold, and it needs no font support at all.

The keys move to the hint line, where they were always going to be taught anyway — and the line has room for all of them now.

M Marks

glyph alone

The most compact, and the only one that would still fit if a fourth and fifth verb ever arrived.

Also the only one you have to learn. Three unlabelled marks on a surface used a few times a week is three things to re-derive every time.

›

U+203A single angle quote. Common in terminal fonts, but not one R0.2 checked on the phone.

!

U+0021 ASCII. Already the expired marker in every row, so it carries the right meaning and cannot be missing.

⌕

U+2315 the search mark. Unverified on Termux — a missing glyph renders as a box, and a box on a button is worse than no button.

Words, in the places that test it

The whole screen, the terminal floor where cells shrink to eleven columns, and the same frame with no colour at all.

What I would build

W — words. Dropping ⏎ Open bought three columns per cell, and the most useful thing to spend them on is legibility, not a second mark. A word needs no font, no legend and no learning, and it is the one label that reads the same to a thumb and to someone who has never opened the app before.

The keys are not lost — they move to the hint line directly under the buttons, which now reads ⏎ open · → detail · ^x scans · ^q quit. That is the right division: the buttons say what happens, the line says how to do it faster.

If you want the visual anchors, G is the same code with a prefix — but I would only take it after checking › and ⌕ on the phone, and ds-spike --glyphs exists for exactly that. A box where a search mark should be is worse than no mark at all.
