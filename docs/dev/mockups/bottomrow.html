R-UI · the bottom two rows

The Last Line

Two changes, and they only make sense as a pair. The count-and-hints row goes above the entry line and takes the band with it; the entry line goes last, on the terminal's own background. The band stops being a marking on the field and becomes a lit rule between the list and the thing you type into — which is what Vim's status line is, sitting exactly where it sits, above a plain command line.

Three arrangements

The two changes only work together. A divider has to be between the things it divides, so the band has to move up, and the entry line has to move down to be under it. Either one alone is worse than what ships today — which is what the middle specimen is there to show.

Why it is the conventional order

Emacs

The minibuffer is the last line of the frame. Nothing is ever drawn beneath it, because there is nothing beneath it.

Vim — exactly this

StatusLine is a highlighted row carrying position and state; : and / take the plain final line beneath it. That is this arrangement, down to which of the two is lit.

fzf

Default layout is bottom-up: results, then the info line 4/24, then the prompt on the last row. The layout we would be moving to is fzf's default, and this surface has been called fzf-shaped since §1.

The thumb

The row you touch most — tap to type, tap the chip — becomes the closest to the bottom edge, and the chip takes the corner every phone app puts its primary action in.

Emacs, again

With the band gone from the entry row, nothing marks the field at all — a prompt and a cursor, which is the minibuffer's own answer and the option the field page recommended before the band was asked for. It arrives here by a different route.

What it fixes

Three problems with the band as built, and all of them are consequences of putting it on the row with your text on it.

As builtWith the band moved up
dim text on a lit row Type to search and For more, hit were dim over a pinned foreground on a grey band — the least legible combination on the screen. They go back onto the plain background.
a pinned foreground The band had to name both ends to survive theme polarity, so your typed text was ANSI 15 rather than your terminal's own foreground. Now it is simply your text, in your colour.
nothing divided the surface The list ran into the chrome with no boundary. The band is now that boundary, which is the job it is actually good at.
the bottom-right cell The caveat evaporates. The last row is plain, so no background is painted into the final cell and there is nothing to make an auto-margin terminal scroll.
NO_COLOR Still loses the band — but it now loses a divider rather than the only thing marking the field, which is a much smaller loss. The prompt and the words were always doing that work.
Unchanged
rowsNone gained, none lost — twenty-one documents browsing, ten while typing.
tap zonesBoth bottom rows still raise the keyboard; an overshoot downwards still hits the screen edge.
the leader sheetStill opens above the chrome, now above the status line.

The states it has to survive

The desktop has the same question

A keyboard layout is query row then hint line. The same swap applies, and the argument is stronger there — a hint line is a status line, and Vim puts the status line above the command line.

What I would build

Swap them, both layouts. I looked for a reason not to and did not find one: no row is gained or lost, no tap zone moves, every state still works, and the resulting layout is what Emacs, Vim and fzf all already do. The band stops floating and becomes a docked bar, which is the thing that prompted the question.

The two costs are real and small. The count reads above the cursor instead of below — fzf's arrangement, so it is well-trodden rather than novel. And messages land above the entry line instead of below; Emacs would put them on it, which we cannot, and above at least keeps the band carrying nothing but your own text.

The one open item is mechanical, not aesthetic: whether painting the bottom-right cell makes the screen scroll. Cheap to see, cheap to fix, and not a reason to hold the change.
