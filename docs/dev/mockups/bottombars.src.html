What the bottom chrome is for

Four jobs. Every direction below is a different answer to which of them deserve a row of their own.

The typing target

One underlined span, fat enough for a thumb, that raises the keyboard. Non-negotiable and device-proven.

Truth about the result

The count, which filters are on, and anything the app needs to say.

Verbs the input can't reach

For a thumb: detail, and two toggles behind modifier keys. For a keyboard: nothing — every verb already has a key.

Teaching

Today this is a hint line that shows a thumb three keyboard verbs, and vanishes entirely once two filters are on.

The directions

Each drawn at exactly 45 columns, on the tiling the app really uses. Two documents of context above every one, so the boundary between list and chrome is comparable.

A Repair in place

Keep the shipped geometry; fix every incoherence the audit found and spend nothing.

One state grammar — a • on the button and nowhere else, so the [expiring] and [scans] chips are deleted. Expiry becomes Expiring everywhere. The hint turns gesture-first: tap again opens ⏎, which is both what a thumb needs and short enough that it can never be squeezed out.

Risk: it leaves the strategy alone. The bar still spends a row on three verbs, one of which the header is scheduled to duplicate — this is maintenance, not design.

B Earn your cell

A verb keeps a cell only if a thumb has no other path to it.

The plan already says the header's counts are tappable. Implement that, and Expiring is evicted from the bar — leaving the two survivors at 21 columns each, the largest touch targets in any direction that keeps a bar.

Risk: a count reads as information even in reverse, so the filter gets harder to find — and its off-switch moves to the top corner, the furthest point from a thumb. Two 21-wide cells around eight letters also look like a bar waiting to die.

C Chips are the buttons recommended

A toggle's control and its state are one object, so draw them once.

The action bar dissolves into the truth row: the chips become pressable, carry their own •, and sit beside the count they modify. The freed row above the field is normally blank and speaks only when there is something to say — a flash, the armed quit, or a one-shot tap the row again to open it. Today a message replaces the chips row; here it has somewhere of its own to go, which is the second specimen.

Risk: the verbs go back to one-row targets — the exact thing two rounds on the device rejected. The mitigations are real (bottom edge below, a harmless neighbour above, all three verbs non-destructive) but they are reasoning, and the two-row rule was measurement.

D The dock

If one row is too small a target for a thumb, that is true for buttons too — so make them two rows tall, beside the field rather than above it.

Field on the left at 21 columns, two 11-column slabs docked right, both two rows deep. Twenty-two cells per verb: the biggest touch targets here by a distance, and the state lives in the slab's own second row.

Risk: the field shrinks to 19 typable columns, and every mis-tap now crosses an internal boundary — left from Detail raises the keyboard, right from the field opens a record. That is precisely the class of error the tiling work removed by putting the row against the screen edge.

E The ascetic field

Chrome is the field and the truth row; detail becomes a gesture on the selected row's right-hand end.

This is the shape the surface wants once a : command line exists — and it is here mostly to mark the floor. Its ⌕ is a three-column target, and the detail gesture rides a row that moves.

Risk: nothing on screen ever teaches the detail gesture, and a thumb three columns off opens the file instead. It proves the minimum is below the right amount of chrome.

not drawn — rejected on structure, not on looks

F The contextual bar rejected

The bar's contents follow the selection: no selection, a document, a pushed record — each gets its own verbs.

This failure is already in the codebase in miniature. Cell 0 swaps → Detail for ← Back when the record opens, while the other cells keep acting on a list the user can no longer see — tapping ^x Expiring inside a pushed record mutates a surface that isn't on screen. A contextual bar multiplies that, on the device with the least precise pointer and no timers to disambiguate with.

The lesson worth keeping: when detail covers the list, the Find chrome should yield entirely. Every direction above should adopt it.

C, in the places that decide it

The whole screen, the same frame with no colour, the 38-column floor, and the keyboard layout — where the same chips reappear on the hint line, one object in both layouts.

What I would build

C, with A's grammar repairs folded in. It fixes the two findings that matter by deleting the objects that caused them rather than policing them: a toggle stops being two things in two textures two rows apart, and the bar stops holding a cell that the header is about to duplicate. It costs no documents, and it is stable when the : line arrives — where the current bar keeps melting, having already lost two of its four cells in two rounds.

The strongest argument against it: it puts touch verbs back on one-row targets, and "one row is too small" is the one thing here that was measured rather than reasoned. If a week of thumb use shows mis-taps, the fallback is D's dock — and only the phone can tell you.

Either way, three fixes are unconditional: the keyboard layout's query row gets its underline, hints degrade item by item instead of vanishing whole, and the Find chrome goes inert while a pushed record covers the list.
