Press and Type

C marked the field with an underline and the prompt with a reverse chip; D marked the buttons with reverse chips. Shipping both would use one texture for two unrelated jobs. So the merge starts by deciding what each texture means — and the four candidates differ only in how strictly that rule is kept.

The rule what makes it one design

A terminal has four things to draw with — reverse video, underline, bold and dim — and they all survive with colour switched off. Cohesion here means each one saying exactly one thing, everywhere, on every surface.

 ⏎ 

Reverse — you can press this. Buttons, and the keyboard affordance at the end of the field. Nothing else.

coc█ 

Underline — you can type here. One span per surface, and only the surface that takes text has one.

3/24 · [scans]

Dim — information only. Counts, chips, hints: things that tell you where you are and never respond to a tap.

Reverse marks the key you press, not the whole button: a cap, then a plain label. Half the ink of M2 for the same affordance, and the bar teaches its own keybindings while it sits there.

It also scales: a fifth button costs a two-column cap, not a whole inverted block.

M3 in the states that matter

An empty field at launch, a toggle switched on, the whole screen, and the same rule on a keyboard layout where there are no buttons at all.

What I would build

M3. It keeps what you liked in C — the underline putting the emphasis exactly where the thumb lands — and what you liked in D, that the buttons look like buttons, while spending a third of D's ink. The cap is also the honest shape: what you press is the key, and on the keyboard layout the same mark teaches the same binding.

One thing to decide with it: the caps are ⏎ → ^x ^t, which means the bar shows control combinations to someone holding a phone with no control key. The alternative is caps that read as touch targets (▸ ▹ ! ⌕) and hints that name the keys instead. I would ship the keys — you use both a keyboard and a thumb on this device, and the keybindings are the part worth learning.

If M3 reads too light on the real screen, M2 is the same code with the cap widened to the whole button; the two differ by one constant.
